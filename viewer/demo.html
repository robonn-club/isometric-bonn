Three looks, one model

Phase 0a asks five members which look reads as a game rather than a GIS map. All three come from the same render; only the colour rules differ.

How it was made

  1. Fetch. {{data_files}} files, {{data_mb}} MB, from OpenGeodata.NRW: LoD2 buildings (2026), DGM1 ground heights (2021), nDOM50 heights above ground and the DOP10 aerial photo (both 2025). The script reads robots.txt first.
  2. Model. {{buildings}} buildings in view, {{triangles}} million triangles: roofs and walls from LoD2, ground and tree crowns as 0.5 m height meshes. Heights count as trees only where the photo's infrared says plants, so cars and signs drop out.
  3. Render. One fixed pixel grid for all of Bonn at {{px_per_m}} px per metre, camera at 30° looking north-east, rasterised in plain NumPy so a tile comes out the same on any machine. Shadows use the photo's own sun, {{sun_az}}° and {{sun_el}}° high, found by matching its building shadows.
  4. Colour. Rules pick from a {{palette}}-colour palette built on the club's Rhein colours; this view uses {{colours}}. The photo picks the shade on ground and low roofs and each roof's colour family; walls and windows follow the building's use.
  5. Tiles. {{tiles}} tiles of 512 px, about {{tile_kb}} KB each as lossless WebP. At that rate all of Bonn, with zoom levels, would need about {{bonn_mb}} MB.

Not there yet

  • Water is read from the photo's infrared, a stand-in for the state's water polygons. The lily pond beside the Schloss is missed.
  • Trees are one surface per canopy, without trunks or single-tree sprites.
  • Wall colours and windows are invented from each building's use. Roofs, ground, trees and heights are measured.
  • No landmarks or robots drawn by members yet.

Checked

  • The Schloss tile rendered on its own matches the same tile in this window, byte for byte: no seams.
  • Every pixel of the two pixel-art looks is a palette colour, also zoomed out.
  • The data reader never reads addresses, and a test confirms none get through.
